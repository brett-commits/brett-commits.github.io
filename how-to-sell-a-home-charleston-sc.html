How to Sell a Home in Charleston, SC

Selling is a pricing and presentation project with a deadline. Here is the order that works, and the one mistake that costs sellers more than any other.

1. Start with the real number

What your home can sell for comes from what similar homes near you actually sold for, not from asking prices or a website estimate. We start with those closed sales, your timeline, and what you need out of the sale, then build the plan around real numbers.

2. Price it right from the start

Your first days on the market are your loudest. Price too high and the home sits, and sitting is what costs you, not the list price you gave up. The data below is blunt about it. The goal is a price that creates competition in week one, not a reduction in week five.

3. Prep where buyers can see it

Fix what an inspector or a buyer will flag anyway, deep clean, declutter, and make the first photo count. Prep money should go where a buyer can see it: light, space, and a home that looks cared for. We will tell you plainly what is worth doing and what is not.

4. Present it and launch it well

Professional photography, an honest description that sells the life in the home, and a launch timed so the listing lands in front of the weekend crowd. Every showing request gets a fast yes. Momentum is a marketing tool.

5. Compare offers on more than price

The highest number is not always the strongest offer. Financing, earnest money, deadlines, contingencies, and the closing date decide how likely an offer is to actually close. We compare them side by side, in writing, and we respond to every serious offer.

6. From contract to closing

Inspections and repair requests, the appraisal, title work, and the closing attorney's steps, with weekly updates from our team until the deed records. If a problem shows up, you hear it from us first, with the options attached.

The cost of sitting, in real numbers

Here is what the last six months of local sales say: the typical home that sat for over a month was listed at $490,000 and sold for $460,000, about $30,000 (roughly 6%) off the original asking price. And the longer it sat, the more came off: on that same $490,000 home, about $26,000 after one month, $34,000 after two, $44,000 after three, $58,000 after four. Most of those sellers were not beaten in negotiation. They were beaten by the calendar. Pricing right at launch is how you keep that money.

Selling to buy new construction?

If your next home is a new build, the timing of this sale against the builder's timeline is the whole game, and there may be options that help with a lease or a gap between homes. Talk to us before you sign anything with a builder, so the plan covers both homes, not just one.

Common questions

When is the best time to sell my home?

Spring brings the most buyers, but the honest answer is: when your home is priced and presented right, and when the move fits your life. A well-priced home sells in every season here. A mispriced one sits in all of them.

How long will it take to sell?

It depends on price, condition, and area. Homes priced at what the recent sales support tend to move in the first few weeks. Homes priced above them join the 30+ days list, where buyers expect a discount.

What does it cost to sell a home?

Costs include agent compensation, which is negotiable and agreed in writing before you list, plus closing costs such as attorney and title charges. Before you sign anything, we build you a net sheet with real numbers for your home, so you know what you would walk away with at different prices.

Should I make repairs before I list?

Some, not all. Safety and water issues and anything an inspector will flag are usually worth handling. Cosmetic projects depend on your price point and timeline. We walk the home with you and sort the list into worth it and skip it.

What would a buyer pay for your home?

Start with a value built on what buyers actually paid near you. It takes about 30 seconds, and a real person reviews every one.
