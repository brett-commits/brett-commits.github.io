How to Buy a Home in Charleston, SC

The buying process, step by step, in plain language: what happens, what it costs, and where buyers lose money when nobody explains it to them.

1. Get clear on the goal and the budget

Start with what the move needs to do for your life: schools, commute, space, yard, payment. Then set a budget you can say out loud, including the monthly payment and the cash you will need at closing. Discovery before data, every time. The right search starts here, not with a map full of dots.

2. Get your financing lined up

Talk to a lender early, before you fall for a house. A real pre-approval is checked paperwork, not a five-minute quiz, and sellers take it seriously. Compare lenders on total cost, loan options, service, and how reliably they close. Your lender gives you the exact numbers for your loan in a Loan Estimate.

3. Search with alerts set up

Set up a saved search so new homes that match come to you by email instead of the other way around. Register, save favorites, and watch the Coming Soon list too: those homes are listed but not yet open for showings, and that head start matters when a good one goes live.

4. Tour a short list that fits

Good tours are short. A focused list of homes that truly fit your criteria, usually a handful, tells you more than a marathon day of maybes. At each home, check the things photos hide: noise, light, storage, the drive to work, and how the street feels at that hour.

5. Make the offer with the full picture

Price is one lever. Earnest money, deadlines, contingencies, and the closing date are the others, and the right mix depends on the home. Before you offer, your agent pulls the home's price history and recent nearby sales. A home that has been sitting tells a different story than one listed yesterday, and your offer should know the difference.

6. From contract to keys

Once you are under contract, the clock runs: inspections during your due diligence period, the appraisal, final loan steps, and a final walk-through before closing. You sign with a closing attorney, the deed records, and you get your keys. Our team sends weekly updates the whole way, so you always know what happens next.

What it costs, in plain terms

Beyond the down payment, plan on closing costs (lender, attorney, and title charges), inspections, and the appraisal, plus earnest money that you put down when the contract is signed and that counts toward your totals at closing. The exact figures depend on your loan and the home, and your lender's Loan Estimate spells them out in writing before you commit. Anyone who quotes you one flat number for every buyer is guessing.

Three mistakes that cost buyers money

Touring before financing

Falling for a home you cannot buy on the timeline the seller wants is the most common heartbreak in this market. Pre-approval first, tours second.

Judging by the list price alone

A home priced high and sitting is not the same as a home priced right and fresh. The list of homes sitting 30+ days is where offers have room to work.

Visiting a builder unrepresented

The friendly agent in a model home works for the builder, not for you. Talk to your own agent before your first visit to any new construction community.

Common questions

How long does it take to buy a home in Charleston?

Finding the home is the variable part: days for some buyers, a few months for others. Once you are under contract, closing often takes about 30 to 45 days, depending on the loan, inspections, and the attorney's schedule.

Do I need a pre-approval before I tour homes?

For most homes, yes. Sellers expect it with an offer, and many will not open the door for tours without one. It also protects you: you shop inside a payment you have already verified.

What does it cost to work with an agent as a buyer?

Compensation is negotiable and is agreed in writing before you tour, so there are no surprises later. We explain exactly how it works in our first conversation, in plain numbers.

What is earnest money?

It is a good-faith deposit you put down when your offer is accepted. It shows the seller you are serious, it is held by the closing attorney or broker, and it is credited back to you at closing as part of your funds.
